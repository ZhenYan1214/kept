## Q0. What the hackathon page says about its structure, judging and timeline (primary source, most important)

### Takeaway
The hackathon is hosted by SMU SCIS together with the GSR Foundation and the University of Málaga (UMA), and runs on DoraHacks. It has an Idea Track and a Tech Track, and the weighted criteria are: social impact 30%, solution quality 25%, meaningful use of Web3 20%, feasibility and sustainability 15%, pitch 10%. **The submission deadline is 2026-10-25.** The final is at SMU on 2026-11-26, the same day as a GSR Foundation–SMU–UMA collaboration signing and the launch of the "2026 Web3 for Social Good White Paper".

### Cited Findings
- Official name: "From Vision to Impact: Building Web3 for Social Good — Collaborate. Innovate. Build for Good 2026". Prize pool USD 20,000. Tags: Web3, Social Impact, Blockchain, AI, Sustainability, Decentralized Identity, DAPP. When checked on 2026-10-14 the page showed 9 Builds and 46 Hackers — [DoraHacks hackathon page](https://dorahacks.io/hackathon/web3-social-good-2026/detail)
- Organizers: "Hosted by Singapore Management University (SMU) in collaboration with the GSR Foundation and the University of Málaga (UMA)". Stated goals: transparency, trust, inclusion, positive social impact — [DoraHacks](https://dorahacks.io/hackathon/web3-social-good-2026/detail)
- Timeline: Aug 26 Launch & Briefing → Sep to Oct 25 Idea & Build Phase (optional mentoring and workshops) → **Oct 26 shortlist announced, finalists invited to SMU Web3 Week** → Oct 27 to Nov 25 Project Refinement (with mentors) → **Nov 26 Final Presentation & Awards**. Submission window on the page: 2026/08/26 to 2026/10/25 00:00 — [DoraHacks](https://dorahacks.io/hackathon/web3-social-good-2026/detail)
- Judging criteria and weights: Social impact potential & problem relevance 30%; Solution quality (concept design / technical execution) 25%; Innovation & meaningful use of Web3 20%; Feasibility & long-term sustainability 15%; Presentation & pitch / demo quality 10% — [DoraHacks](https://dorahacks.io/hackathon/web3-social-good-2026/detail)
- Judging panel: "Representatives from GSR Foundation, SMU, UMA, and other invited experts". No individual names are given — [DoraHacks](https://dorahacks.io/hackathon/web3-social-good-2026/detail)
- Nov 26 programme (08:30–17:00 SGT, room TBC). Morning: 09:15 **GSR Foundation–SMU–UMA collaboration signing ceremony**; 09:35 **launch of the 2026 Web3 for Social Good White Paper**; 10:00 "Invited Case Presentations" (selected projects and practical applications featured in the white paper); 11:00 panel with SMU, UMA, GSR Foundation, industry and the public sector on "pathways to real-world social impact". Afternoon: 13:00 Idea Track finals; 14:30 Tech Track finals; 16:30 awards — [DoraHacks](https://dorahacks.io/hackathon/web3-social-good-2026/detail)
- Eligibility: university students and researchers, Web3 developers, entrepreneurs and social innovators, and non-technical participants — [DoraHacks](https://dorahacks.io/hackathon/web3-social-good-2026/detail)
- **Idea Track**: suggested areas are sustainability and climate, inclusion and community empowerment, financial inclusion, education and healthcare, public goods and civic participation, humanitarian aid and charitable giving. A submission must explain the social challenge, the intended users or beneficiaries, the solution, how Web3 adds meaningful value, and the potential impact. **No prototype, code or demo video is needed at the first stage.** Shortlisted teams submit a final Idea Description by **2026-11-23** and then receive "AI-assisted development (Vibe Coding)" support to build a prototype — [DoraHacks Tracks](https://dorahacks.io/hackathon/web3-social-good-2026/tracks)
- **Tech Track**: example builds are transparent donation and fund-tracking tools, community and public-goods funding platforms, humanitarian aid distribution, digital identity and verifiable credentials, community governance, climate apps, and AI + Web3. Teams can build on Ethereum/EVM, Solana, Aptos, Sui or other public chains, and may use AI, decentralized storage, identity, ZK proofs and oracles. The final can be presented **online or in person** — [DoraHacks Tracks](https://dorahacks.io/hackathon/web3-social-good-2026/tracks)

### Inferences
- Social impact and problem relevance (30%) outweighs technical execution, and "meaningful use of Web3" (20%) suggests judges will ask "why does this need blockchain?". The pitch must state clearly why Web3 is necessary.
- The final day centres on a white paper launch and case presentations, and the panel includes the public sector. This points to judges preferring projects with a credible deployment path (NGO or government partners, pilots) over pure tech demos.
- Aptos and Sui are named as supported chains. SMU holds an Aptos-funded professorship (see Q2), so building on Aptos Move may get a small extra nod from SMU judges. This is not stated anywhere.
- Online final presentations are allowed, which lowers the barrier for a Taiwan-based team.

### Gaps
- No judge names, tracks-specific prize split, or white paper preview text found. The Builds tab showed "No Builds" to a logged-out viewer, so competing projects could not be checked.

## Q1. GSR Foundation: mission, focus areas, grants and partners

### Takeaway
The GSR Foundation is a UK-registered charity (no. 1199228) founded with a USD 10M pledge from the crypto market maker GSR. It uses proactive, invitation-based grantmaking in three focus areas: Blockchain for Good, Education Infrastructure and Internet Provision/Access. The hackathon page also adds digital public goods and responsible emerging tech. In 2026 its most concrete social-good action was co-funding UMA's "Blockchain for Good · Proof of Aid" hackathon, which was about NGO donation traceability.

### Cited Findings
- Hackathon page self-description: "independent global charity and grant-maker established with support from GSR". Mission: "help ensure that technological progress includes everyone". Focus areas: Blockchain for Good, digital public goods, education infrastructure, internet access, and responsible applications of emerging technology — [DoraHacks](https://dorahacks.io/hackathon/web3-social-good-2026/detail)
- Launched in Feb 2023 (older) with a USD 10M pledge from GSR to address "technological barriers encountered by marginalized communities" — [The Block, 2023-02-16](https://www.theblock.co/post/212278/gsr-pledges-10-million-to-its-philanthropic-social-impact-foundation); [CorpGov](https://corpgov.com/gsr-launches-foundation-with-10m-pledge-celebrating-10-years-of-business/)
- England & Wales charity number 1199228 — [OpenCharities PDF](https://opencharities.uk/ew/1199228.pdf)
- Grantmaking is proactive: the foundation seeks out partners rather than running open calls, and funds flexibly, including overhead and salaries. A third-party funder directory (unofficial, unverified) gives grant sizes of about £30k–75k ("Enable") and £250k ("Empower") — [Hinchilla funder directory](https://www.hinchilla.com/funder-directory/the-gsr-foundation)

This could not be fetched directly to verify — [gsrfoundation.io](https://www.gsrfoundation.io/)
- Other headlines and partners from search snippets (dates unverified): a partnership with LMAX and The Considered Ask to add crypto education to Money Ready programmes; a partnership with The Visiola Foundation; a UNICEF Venture Fund × GSR Foundation fireside on catalytic capital — [GSR insights](https://www.gsr.io/insights/unicef-venture-fund-and-gsr-foundation-spotlight-catalytic-capital-in-london-fireside-chat); [GSR Foundation LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/company/gsr-foundation)

### Inferences
- GSR Foundation's interests: (1) financial and digital inclusion for marginalised communities; (2) blockchain in humanitarian and charity work (UNICEF CryptoFund, Mercy Corps Ventures); (3) education and connectivity infrastructure.
- The Málaga "Proof of Aid" theme suggests the white paper and the judges are especially interested in **donation and aid traceability that also protects beneficiary privacy**. That points to designs such as ZK, selective disclosure or keeping personal data off-chain. A Taiwan team in a similar space will be compared directly with the Málaga finalist, so it needs to differentiate, for example on local scenarios, stablecoin disbursement, or digital-identity verification.
- As a charity, GSR Foundation likely values sustainable operating models over token speculation. This matches the 15% "Feasibility & long-term sustainability" weight.

His interests are Web3, collaborative intelligence, data assets and AI governance, and RegTech. SMU's first "Web3 Week" (Nov 2025) was co-run with the Sim Kee Boon Institute (SKBI) and included a DePIN + Green Economy panel.

It ended with a hackathon (Aptos X SMU) in which six student teams pitched to experts from SMU, NUS, Aptos, Moledao and others — [SMU News, 2026-01-23](https://news.smu.edu.sg/news/2026/01/23/shaping-future-web3)

Projects that address compliance, for example how a donation flow meets AML rules or how beneficiary data is governed, may score better.
- The hackathon tags include AI, and the Idea Track offers vibe-coding support. An AI + Web3 combination, such as AI auditing of donation flows or AI fraud detection, is likely welcome.

### Gaps

## Q5. Singapore: MAS policy and social problems

### Takeaway
MAS policy experiments relevant to social good include **Purpose Bound Money (PBM)** from Project Orchid, which programs conditions on how money can be used and fits targeted aid and vouchers; Project Guardian (tokenisation); and the Single-Currency Stablecoin framework. Singapore's social problems: it became a **super-aged society** in 2026 (21.4% of citizens aged 65+), it relies heavily on migrant workers (1.98M non-residents), and digital giving is growing fast (Giving.sg S$104.9M in 2025).

### Cited Findings
- Project Orchid explores a retail CBDC. Its first phase produced **Purpose Bound Money (PBM)**, "a protocol that specifies the conditions upon which an underlying digital currency can be used" — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/348509/singapore-to-roll-out-stablecoin-regulations-expand-cbdc-trials?rand=52368)
- Project Guardian has tested tokenised FX, fixed income and funds since 2022, with 40+ financial institutions by late 2025. MAS planned to pilot tokenised government bills settled in wholesale CBDC in 2026 — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/348509/singapore-to-roll-out-stablecoin-regulations-expand-cbdc-trials?rand=52368); [Central Banking](https://www.centralbanking.com/central-banks/currency/digital-currencies/7974086/mas-to-issue-tokenised-bills-and-draft-stablecoin-law)
- Stablecoins (late 2025, older): MAS finalised its regime (stability, capital, redemption at par, disclosure) for stablecoins issued in Singapore and pegged to SGD or a G10 currency. Only licensed issuers may use the label "MAS-regulated stablecoin". MAS said it would draft legislation — [Central Banking](https://www.centralbanking.com/central-banks/currency/digital-currencies/7974086/mas-to-issue-tokenised-bills-and-draft-stablecoin-law); [Singapore Law Watch](https://www.singaporelawwatch.sg/Headlines/MAS-seeks-feedback-on-proposals-regulating-value-user-protection-for-stablecoins)
- Ageing: as of June 2026, 21.4% of citizens were 65+ (13.7% in 2016), crossing the UN "super-aged" threshold of 21%. Citizens aged 80+ grew from 95,000 to 152,000. Seniors aged 65+ living alone: 87,000 in 2024 (58,000 in 2018) — [Eco-Business](https://www.eco-business.com/news/singapore-got-old-fast-now-rethinking-who-cares-for-its-people/); [Mothership, 2026-09](https://mothership.sg/2026/09/singapore-population-2026)
- Migrant workers: total population 6.21M (June 2026). Non-residents rose 3.7% to 1.98M, driven mainly by Work Permit holders (construction) and migrant domestic workers. Ageing also increases demand for care workers — [Malay Mail, 2026-09-26](https://www.malaymail.com/news/singapore/2026/09/26/singapore-gets-bigger-as-foreign-workers-drive-population-growth-and-much-older/236569); [HR Online](https://www.humanresourcesonline.net/singapore-s-total-population-grew-to-6-21mn-as-at-june-2026)
- Giving.sg received a record S$104.9M in donations in 2025, up 21% year on year (secondary source) — [Eco-Business](https://www.eco-business.com/news/singapore-got-old-fast-now-rethinking-who-cares-for-its-people/)

### Inferences
- PBM (programmable, purpose-limited money) is the best Singapore policy hook for disaster relief, welfare vouchers and conditional cash transfers. A project that frames itself as "PBM-style conditional stablecoin aid" is easy to explain to SMU and public-sector judges.
- Migrant domestic workers (MDWs) are a cross-border theme shared by Singapore and Taiwan: remittances, skills credentials, wage-payment proof. One design could serve both places.

### Gaps
- Project Greenprint, Charity Portal transparency rules, Commissioner of Charities data, and Singapore Web3 social-impact startups were not researched due to tool-call limits. The 2026 status of the stablecoin bill was not confirmed.

## Q6. Taiwan: policy, regulation, social problems and Web3 social-good projects

### Takeaway
Taiwan's best hooks: (1) the **TW DIW (數位憑證皮夾)** entered pilot operation in December 2025 and uses W3C VC with selective disclosure, aligned with the EU EUDI; (2) the **虛擬資產服務法 (Virtual Asset Service Act) passed its third reading on 2026-06-30**, which brings stablecoin issuance under licensing; (3) about **870–880k migrant workers** remit around NT$84.2 billion a year through licensed small-remittance providers; (4) the 2025 Hualien Guangfu barrier-lake flood raised over NT$1.19 billion and triggered a crisis of trust in local-government donations.

### Cited Findings
**Digital identity / TW DIW**
- MODA: TW DIW is "neither an e-wallet nor a digital ID card" but a carrier for credentials, and not a payment tool. Users decide what to store and what to disclose (selective disclosure) — [MODA wallet page](https://moda.gov.tw/en/major-policies/wallet/1695)
- 2025-12-17: MODA released the 數位憑證皮夾 app for pilot operation in six scenarios: digital access, car rental, graduation/job search, convenience-store parcel pickup, visitor management, and online applications. Parcel pickup came first (three telecom carriers and FamilyMart). It cannot hold credit cards — [iThome](https://www.ithome.com.tw/news/173835); [工商時報 2025-12-17](https://www.ctee.com.tw/news/20251217701748-430303)
- 2026 plans: open downloads of driver's licences, business certificates and diplomas, and expand scenarios. NCKU diplomas were expected in Q1 2026; 1111 and 104 job banks were expected to support it in Q2 and Q4. Official site: wallet.gov.tw. No confirmed date for a full official launch — [iThome](https://www.ithome.com.tw/news/173835); [NTUST campus notice PDF, 2026-06](https://www.academic.ntust.edu.tw/var/file/48/1048/img/2569/582502618.pdf)
- MODA held an international forum linking TW DIW to the EU EUDI Wallet framework (2025) — [數位時代 BNext](https://www.bnext.com.tw/article/85565/digital-wallet-2025)
- Unconfirmed: an investment blog says an expanded version launched in August 2026 combining government credentials and private membership cards. Not verified against MODA — [Wistock](https://blog.wistock.ai/?p=30518)

**Crypto regulation**
- **2026-06-30, third reading of the 《虛擬資產服務法》 (Virtual Asset Service Act)**: Taiwan's first dedicated crypto law, 56 articles, regulator FSC. It creates a VASP licensing regime with 7 service types (exchange, trading platform, transfer, custody, underwriting, lending, etc.) and **brings stablecoin issuance under licensing** (central bank consultation, reserve assets, segregation, redemption, disclosure). Market manipulation carries up to 10 years in prison — [風傳媒](https://www.storm.mg/articles/1145985); [Inside](https://www.inside.com.tw/article/41702-virtual-asset-services-act); [鏡週刊/三立 2026-06-30](https://www.mirrormedia.mg/external/setn_1864839)
- Commencement will be set by the Executive Yuan after FSC finishes secondary regulations. Estimates for entry into force vary: one says as early as 2027, another says nine sets of secondary regulations as early as Q1 next year, with a two-year transition. Sources differ — [風傳媒](https://www.storm.mg/articles/1145985); [遠見](https://www.gvm.com.tw/article/131843)

**Migrant workers**
- MOL: over 870k migrant workers at end-January 2026 (Indonesia about 330k, Vietnam about 290k, Philippines about 180k, Thailand 70k+). About 873k at end-March 2026; about 880k reported for end-April 2026. Household caregivers are about 70% Indonesian. Migrant workers are roughly 7% of employment. India pilot: about 1,000 workers, mainly manufacturing, possibly from late 2026 (controversial) — [PTS 公視](https://news.pts.org.tw/article/803289); [Newtalk 2026-04-15](https://newtalk.tw/news/view/2026-04-15/1029916); [自由時報](https://ec.ltn.com.tw/article/breakingnews/5415292)

### Inferences
- **Problems a Taiwan team could build around (matched to judges' interests):**
  1. **Verifiable disaster-relief fund flows**: the Hualien cases provide real trust failures, a central foundation that has promised auditability, and a precedent of crypto donations. Combine on-chain fund tracking with TW DIW-style VC selective disclosure to verify victim eligibility without exposing personal data. This matches both GSR's "Proof of Aid" interest and the TW DIW policy direction.
  2. **Migrant worker inclusion**: verifiable credentials for skills and wage payment, plus low-cost stablecoin remittances. ## Q7. Southeast Asia: crypto remittances and financial inclusion

### Takeaway
The Philippines is the most mature market for stablecoin remittances: about USD 35.6B in 2025 cash remittances, Coins.ph's PHPC peso stablecoin, and a Coins.ph–FinFan corridor with Vietnam. Indonesia and Vietnam (the largest migrant groups in Taiwan) are the main origin countries for Taiwan's migrant workers, so a Taiwan-to-SEA corridor is a natural theme.

### Cited Findings
- Overseas Filipino workers sent a record USD 35.63B in cash remittances in 2025 (+3.3%). High fees and slow transfers push users toward digital options — [NewsBytes PH, 2025-12-11](https://newsbytes.ph/2025/12/11/stablecoins-emerging-as-alternative-for-global-remittances-among-pinoys/)
- Coins.ph's peso stablecoin PHPC can be integrated into merchant POS — [Kapronasia](https://kapronasia.com/insight/blogs/blockchain-research/coins-ph-launches-regulated-stablecoin-in-the-philippines)
- Nov 2025: Coins.ph and Vietnam's FinFan signed an MoU. Coins.ph converts funds to USD on licensed rails, then sends a fiat international remittance to FinFan for local payout (a hybrid model) — [TechNode Global, 2025-11-18](https://technode.global/2025/11/18/coins-ph-and-finfan-partner-to-support-philippines-vietnam-remittances-with-new-technology/)
- Industry analysis claims Coins.ph × BCRemit's USDC/USDT route cuts fees by about 80%, and that 77–79% of adults in the Philippines, Vietnam and Indonesia lack full banking access. Both are advocacy or secondary figures and unverified — [Spark research](https://www.spark.money/research/stablecoin-southeast-asia-corridor-growth)

### Inferences
- The Taiwan-to-Indonesia/Vietnam/Philippines migrant remittance corridor (NT$84B+ a year) plus regulated stablecoins (Taiwan's new law, MAS framework, PHPC) gives a regionally relevant and policy-aligned story. Real fee savings must be demonstrated rather than assumed.

### Gaps
- No reliable 2026 data on Indonesian or Vietnamese crypto remittances. Indonesia's regulatory status (OJK) was not researched.
